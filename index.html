Z 0.2Origin

Two childhood friends, a parts business, then printers

Anycubic grew out of a venture that sold electronic modules and spare parts to the DIY 3D-printing scene. The founders noticed parts demand was outrunning anything else and moved into complete machines. Their first breakout was the i3 Mega (2016), an affordable FDM printer that became one of the best-selling budget machines on Amazon. The Photon line then made resin printing cheap enough for hobbyists and won Red Dot and iF design awards.

Lu (Bryant) OuyangFounder and CEO. Built the company around cross-border e-commerce from day one.
Dr. James OuyangCo-founder and CTO. Materials-science PhD from the University of Auckland; leads R&D and is the company's technical voice in the community.

Headcount estimates range from about 300 to about 1,000 depending on the source.

Z 0.8Market

A crowded top four, led by Bambu Lab

Four Chinese brands ship about 90% of all printers under $2,500. Anycubic sits in the second tier with Elegoo, well behind the two leaders. Demand keeps growing, but price wars are squeezing margins: Creality issued a profit warning in 2026.

Rival

Bambu Lab

The benchmark for "it just works". Premium ecosystem, MakerWorld library, fast-growing share leader.

Rival

Creality

The old volume leader, open-source roots, pursuing a Hong Kong IPO and diversifying.

Rival

Elegoo

Anycubic's closest twin: strong in resin, aggressive on price.

Z 1.0Brand

How they talk and how they market

For Freedom to Make.

The brand line, also used as the name of their education program. The positioning rests on three words the company uses about itself: simpler, more practical, smarter. The pitch is democratization: a capable printer for an ordinary budget.

Anniversary sales

Their biggest annual moment

Every late summer. 2025 "Love and Share"; 2026 "11 Years, 11 Million Ideas: Make-It-Real" with discounts of up to $550.

Launch funnel

Deposit → early bird → launch weeks

Kobra X ran a $10 deposit phase, then early-bird, then "Fantastic Weeks". Photon P1 used Kickstarter.

Community

Makeronline challenges and points

Creators earn points for sharing projects. A yearly Fan Festival. Reddit AMAs with executives.

Partnerships

Content as a moat

The Thingiverse deal answers Bambu's MakerWorld: more printable things, ready in multicolor.

Channels

Amazon, own store, affiliates

Regional web stores (US, EU, AU, CA), heavy Amazon presence, an affiliate and YouTube-creator program.

Trade shows

Formnext, RAPID+TCT

Used to unveil flagships and reach prosumers and press.

Z 1.2Watch-outs

What the community remembers

  • The 2024 hack.

    Printers worldwide showed a "hacked_machine_readme" file. Anycubic was slow to respond publicly. Any campaign about cloud or app features should expect this to come up.

  • Uneven support.

    Trustpilot and Reddit are mixed: some praise, many complaints about slow tickets and deliveries.

  • Seen as the value option.

    Frequent deep discounts help volume but train buyers to wait for a sale and keep the brand in the "budget alternative" slot.

  • Closed vs open.

    Hackers and enthusiasts push for open firmware. Messaging on openness and ownership is a sensitive area.

Z 1.4For our campaign
Openings

Where a campaign could push

  • Resin authority. Photon is their clearest point of difference against Bambu, which is FDM-first.
  • Multicolor for everyone. Kobra X makes four-color printing a sub-$300 purchase.
  • Ideas, not machines. Thingiverse plus AnyLab lets the story move from "printer" to "what you make".
  • Community voice. 5M users and Makeronline creators are a ready source of real stories.
Ask the client

Questions for the kickoff

  1. Which product or line is the campaign for, and which market (US, EU, global)?
  2. Goal: launch awareness, sales during a sale window, or brand repositioning?
  3. Is AnyLab launching soon, and can we show it?
  4. Brand guidelines, tone of voice, and approved claims (e.g. "#1 in Europe")?
  5. Budget, timing, and which channels they own today.
